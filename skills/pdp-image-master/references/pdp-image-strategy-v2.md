# PDP Image Strategy v2 - Independent-Site Conversion

Use this reference for premium independent-site PDP image sets, especially men's gifts, cigar accessories, barware, home-bar, lifestyle, travel, leather-look goods, and other products where conversion depends on taste, desire, trust, and identity.

## Core Rule

PDP images are not product explanations. Their job is to increase conversion.

Before creating any PDP image, ask:

- Is this image merely introducing the product?
- Or is it helping the shopper want to own the product?

If the answer is the first, redesign the image.

Do not make PDP images feel like parameter manuals, low-end Amazon infographics, feature dumps, engineering diagrams, white-background cutouts, or self-congratulatory seller claims. Design around the buyer's purchase motivation.

## Copy Principle

Copy should change how shoppers judge the product. Translate seller-side features into buyer-side reasons.

Do not explain only what the product is. Explain why it is worth owning.

Use this translation pattern:

```text
attribute -> function -> buyer benefit
```

Examples:

- Do not stop at "Premium leather." Translate to "Protects your collection anywhere" when evidence supports the protection claim.
- Do not stop at "Built-in hygrometer." Translate to "Keeps every cigar fresh for the moment you earned it" only when humidity-control evidence supports the claim.

## Five-Layer Conversion Model

Apply these layers before writing image copy or prompts:

1. **Customer task:** What practical job does this solve, and why should the shopper care?
2. **Scene:** What future moment does this product let the shopper imagine owning?
3. **Emotion:** What feeling does it carry: ritual, reward, relaxation, achievement, private time, taste, or confidence?
4. **Product voice:** When useful, let the product speak like a trusted companion instead of a seller shouting claims.
5. **Identity:** Highest priority. What kind of person owns this product?

Identity examples:

- "For Men Who Appreciate The Ritual"
- "A Gentleman's Essential"
- "A Symbol Of Taste"

Use identity language only when it fits the product and brand. Do not force it onto utilitarian products where trust or proof matters more.

## Independent-Site Visual Rules

Use independent-site and lifestyle-brand taste as the benchmark: Bespoke Post, Huckberry, Carl Friedrik, Davidoff, Shinola, Saddleback Leather, Ridge, Case Elegance.

Do not imitate 1688, Taobao, Pinduoduo, or low-price Amazon seller graphics.

Independent-site PDPs should prioritize emotional density over information density. Shoppers often buy because the product feels desirable, giftable, useful, and identity-relevant, not because every parameter is on the image.

## Layout Discipline

Every image needs breathing room.

Prefer:

- one scene
- one sentence
- one emotion
- one core message

Avoid full-screen arrows, dense callouts, crowded copy, fake badges, and visual clutter.

Suggested text density:

- Hero image: one strong line or one short supporting line
- Function image: no more than three information points
- Scene image: one sentence
- UGC image: natural trust cue, not advertising copy
- Identity close: one brand-level statement

## Image Generation Rules

Avoid:

- cutout/pasted-on product feel
- ecommerce white-background feel
- cheap 3D render feel
- engineering/spec-sheet feel
- hard-sell advertising feel
- low-end infographic feel

Require:

- realistic photography feel
- editorial or magazine-grade composition
- cinematic lifestyle scenes where appropriate
- warm light and believable shadows
- real material texture
- real depth of field
- real environments and human context when useful
- subtle signs of use, scale, and life

When AI generation is used, prefer native scene generation with the product integrated into lighting and perspective. Use deterministic scripts for final text, specs, contact sheets, QA, claims audits, and metadata, not as the main visual substitute for premium scenes.

## UGC Rules

UGC-style images must not look like ads. Their job is trust.

UGC should feel like a real user moment:

- natural capture
- no direct camera performance unless the scene calls for it
- non-model feel
- slight imperfection
- realistic environment
- appropriate age and lifestyle fit

Preferred men's lifestyle scenes:

- after golf
- backyard patio
- lake cabin
- weekend getaway
- business travel
- private study

## Preferred 8-Image Structure

For men's lifestyle products, prefer this structure unless the product category or user brief requires another order:

1. **Hero:** first impression and desire
2. **Pain point:** why the shopper needs it
3. **Material:** quality and tactile proof
4. **Core function:** solve the main objection or usage question
5. **Structure:** design logic, parts, profile, or mechanism
6. **Scene:** let the shopper imagine future ownership
7. **UGC/trust:** make the product feel real and owned
8. **Identity:** raise the product from commodity to lifestyle symbol

This sequence is not a rigid template. Each image still needs one dominant buyer-facing job and evidence-safe copy.

## Final Standard

Do not sell the product alone. Sell the lifestyle, identity, and feeling of owning it.

Do not sell functions alone. Sell the buyer's reason to care.

Do not sell materials alone. Sell what owning those materials says and feels like, while keeping all factual claims evidence-safe.
